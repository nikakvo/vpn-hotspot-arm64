1 · what this module does

Android's VPN protects the apps on your phone only. Devices connected to your hotspot (or USB / Bluetooth tethering) are sent straight to your mobile or Wi-Fi connection, around the VPN: a laptop on your hotspot shows your carrier's address, not the VPN's.

This module sends everything those devices do through the phone's VPN. With the kill switch on, they never reach the internet any other way: if the VPN drops, they are cut off until it is back.

works with
VPN appsAny app that uses Android's VPN: root WireGuard (kernel backend) and VpnService apps such as v2rayNG, Proton VPN or OpenVPN. Interface names like tun0 or wg-… are followed automatically. Tested on the device: WireGuard (kernel backend) and v2rayNG.
TetheringWi-Fi hotspot, USB, Bluetooth — several at once.
RootSukiSU Ultra, KernelSU, APatch or Magisk (WebUI through MMRL / KSU WebUI Standalone).
2 · quick start
  1. Flash the zip, reboot.
  2. Connect your VPN, turn the hotspot on, connect a device.
  3. Open this WebUI: the route at the top shows Clients → your VPN → Internet with a moving line.
  4. On the device, open ipleak.net or a "what is my IP" page: it shows the VPN's address.
  5. Turn the VPN off for a moment: the device loses internet (kill switch), and gets it back when the VPN reconnects — no action needed.
3 · how it works

↓

The pieces

Per hotspot interface the module adds routing rules just above Android's own tethering rule (priority 20400–20600, Android's is 21000), a firewall chain that only lets devices out through the VPN, address translation into the tunnel, a TCP packet-size limit for the tunnel, and a block on forwarded IPv6.

Watchdog

A small background loop keeps all of this true while things change: hotspot on/off, a new hotspot subnet, the VPN reconnecting (Android gives it a new routing table each time), a server switch (new interface name), a new MTU, Android reordering or flushing the firewall. It listens for network events and reacts about 2 seconds after they settle; every 10 seconds it also checks what produces no event.

Living next to Android

While a VPN connects, Android's network daemon (netd) changes the firewall a lot. The module waits until that burst is over, reads each table once and writes all its changes in one step, so netd never has to wait for it. A hotspot that appears in the meantime is blocked at once with a routing rule that does not touch the firewall.

4 · states and the banner

blockedNo VPN, kill switch on: devices have no internet. Connect the VPN.
directNo VPN, kill switch off: devices use your connection directly, like without the module.
idleHotspot is off. Nothing is installed.

"Rules changed by the system — repairing" means Android or another module changed the firewall; the watchdog puts it back within seconds.

5 · dashboard

Route

Where hotspot traffic goes right now. Moving yellow line: through the VPN. Red cut: kill switch. Orange: straight to your connection.

Exit

Check asks an IP service (ip-api.com, ipinfo.io as fallback) once, through the VPN — the same way the devices go — and shows the address, place and provider websites see. Nothing is sent until you tap it.

Protection

Each piece, checked against the live firewall on every watchdog pass: ok in place, repairing missing or moved (fixed on the next pass), not used not needed in this state (for example IPv6 on a device without it).

Devices

Wi-Fi devices come from the hotspot's own station list (the same count Android shows); USB and Bluetooth devices from the neighbour table.

6 · settings
setting
Route through VPNMaster switch. Off removes every rule at once.
Kill switchOn (default): no VPN → no internet for devices. Off: no VPN → devices use your connection directly.
Detect a dead tunnelOn (default): if the phone keeps sending into the VPN for 20 s and nothing comes back, the module pings 1.1.1.1 / 9.9.9.9 through the tunnel. No answer → the VPN counts as down (kill switch on: devices blocked; off: devices use your connection) until it answers again. Catches a VPN that is "connected" but dead, e.g. after switching from Wi-Fi to mobile data.
VPN interfaceAuto (default) follows the VPN Android has connected. Choose a name only when two VPNs run at once.
DNS fallbackOnly used when no local resolver handles DNS (DNSCrypt Proxy missing or paused). Default Quad9 9.9.9.9, always reached through the tunnel.

Check now compares the rules with what should be there and repairs. Rebuild puts everything back from scratch. Report collects the state, all rules and the log in one text you can copy.

7 · with dnscrypt proxy and ipset

Tested on Poco F6 Pro, Xiaomi.eu ROM (HyperOS 3, Android 16), kernel GKI_Kernel_SukiSU.

The three modules are one set. Their firewall order is fixed and checked on every pass: DNSCrypt's hotspot filter first, then ipset's IP blocklists, then this module's tunnel rules, then Android's. A device can never reach the tunnel around the two filters.

DNS: with DNSCrypt Proxy installed, a device asks the phone, DNSCrypt answers — encrypted, filtered, and itself sent through the VPN. Without it (or while it is paused) the module sends devices' DNS to the fallback server through the tunnel, so it never goes to your carrier's resolver.

You can remove the DNS = line from your WireGuard configs: with DNSCrypt Proxy, DNS never needs the VPN provider's resolver.
8 · IPv6, speed and other limits

IPv6

Devices' IPv6 is not forwarded, so nothing leaks around an IPv4-only tunnel. Devices fall back to IPv4 at once (they get a rejection, not a timeout).

Speed

The module adds no measurable cost; the tunnel and the hotspot radio set the limit. A 2.4 GHz hotspot tops out near 100 Mbit/s — set the hotspot to 5 GHz in Android's hotspot settings if your devices support it.

Offload

Android can speed up tethering by bypassing the firewall (tether offload). It only does so for traffic to your normal connection, never for traffic sent into the VPN, so no setting needs changing.

10 · troubleshooting

Devices have no internet

Is the state blocked? Then the VPN is not connected — the kill switch is doing its job. Connect the VPN, or turn the kill switch off.

A device shows my real address

Check the state is via vpn and every Protection row is ok. Open Report and look at "route for client": it must say dev followed by your VPN interface.

A guest's phone has no internet (Private DNS)

A phone with Private DNS set to a hostname (Android: Settings → Private DNS, e.g. dns.adguard-dns.com) accepts no other DNS. With DNSCrypt Proxy's Block DoT for hotspot clients on, that phone gets no DNS at all. Either the guest sets Private DNS to Automatic (the phone then uses your DNSCrypt, filtered), or you turn that switch off in DNSCrypt Proxy (their encrypted DNS then works — still through your VPN, but without your filters). Encrypted DNS cannot be redirected to yours.

After a server switch a device still shows the old address

The module ends every client connection when the VPN changes server, so they reopen through the new one. A page may still show a cached result — reload it.

Connections opened while devices went out directly

While devices use your connection directly (module off, or kill switch off without VPN), Android accelerates their connections past the firewall. The moment the tunnel takes over again, the module ends those connections (a small helper, bin/vhs-ctflush); devices reopen them at once, through the VPN. The log shows how many were ended.

The VPN app fails to connect or disconnect

Turn the module off in Settings, then retry. If the VPN now works, send the Report and the VPN app's own log. If it still fails, reboot: Android may have kept a half-removed VPN network.

Watchdog not running

Reboot. If it stays stopped, the log (Log tab, or /data/adb/vpn-hotspot.log) says why.
